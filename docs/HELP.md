## Getting started

*What the app does and how to begin.*

Hearing Aid turns any earphones into a personal sound amplifier. The phone's microphone (or your
headset's) picks up the sound around you, the app shapes it to your hearing, and plays it into
your ears with very little delay.

1. Install the app and open it. Read the safety notice and tap **I understand**. You can choose
   the app's language right there.
2. Allow the microphone when Android asks. The app cannot work without it.
3. Connect your earphones. The app will not start without them, because the phone's loudspeaker
   would cause loud feedback.
4. Take the **Hearing test** once (about 5 minutes) so the sound is tuned to your ears.
5. On **Listen**, set the volume low and tap **START**. Raise the volume slowly until speech is
   comfortable and clear.

## Choosing earphones

| Earphones | How they sound |
|---|---|
| Wired (3.5 mm or USB-C) | The most natural. No extra delay, so voices match lips. **Best choice.** |
| Bluetooth (AirPods, Galaxy Buds and others) | Work well, but Bluetooth adds about 0.15–0.3 s of delay. Voices may not match lips, and your own voice can sound echoey. |
| Bluetooth LE Audio and Android hearing aids | Supported where the phone supports them. |

Earphones that seal the ear canal (in-ear tips) reduce whistling and block more room noise than
open earbuds.

## The hearing test

*Tunes the app to your ears. Take it in a quiet room with the earphones you will use.*

1. Open **Hearing test** and tap **Start test**. The app sets the phone volume itself during the
   test and puts it back afterwards.
2. You hear short beeps at six pitches (250 Hz to 8 kHz), first in the right ear, then in the left.
3. Tap the big **I HEAR IT** button every time you hear a beep, even if it is very faint. Don't
   guess; the pauses between beeps are random on purpose.
4. When it finishes, your result appears as an audiogram (red O = right ear, blue X = left ear;
   lower on the chart means you need louder sound at that pitch), and the app tunes itself.

The test uses ordinary earphones, which are not calibrated like clinical equipment. It measures
the *shape* of your hearing (which pitches and which ear need more help), not exact hearing levels.
You can take it again at any time, for example with different earphones.

## Listen: volume, balance and situations

- **START / STOP** turns the hearing aid on and off. The card above it shows which earphones and
  microphone are in use and how much delay the phone adds.
- **Loudness** sets the overall volume.
- **Balance** makes the left or right ear louder.
- **Situation** adjusts the sound for where you are:

| Situation | What it does |
|---|---|
| Everyday | Your own tuning, unchanged |
| Conversation | A little more of the pitches that make speech clear |
| Noisy place | Less low rumble, softer loud sounds, stronger noise reduction |
| TV & music | Fuller sound with less compression |

- **Microphone**: *Automatic* lets Android choose, *Phone mic* always uses the phone, *Headset mic*
  uses the microphone of wired or USB earphones.
- **Tip:** put the phone near the person speaking, on the table or in their hand. It then works like
  a remote microphone and you hear them clearly over room noise.

## Noise and whistling

**Reduce background noise** lowers steady noise such as fans, air conditioning, traffic, engines
and hum. Speech, which changes all the time, passes through. It cannot remove other people talking.

**Whistling** (feedback) means sound from the earphones leaks back into the microphone. To stop it:

- lower the volume,
- push the earphones further into your ears or use in-ear tips,
- keep the phone away from your head, or put it on a table near the speaker.

## Adjust: fine-tuning each pitch

The Adjust tab has a slider for each of six pitches, for both ears together or for each ear.

- Raise a pitch if voices sound **muffled or unclear**. Clarity of consonants (s, f, th) lives at
  2–4 kHz.
- Lower a pitch if sound is **sharp, tinny or harsh**.
- **Loud-sound softening** keeps sudden loud sounds (a door, a dish) comfortable while quiet sounds
  still get full gain.
- **Maximum loudness** is a hard safety limit: nothing ever comes out louder than this.
- **Phone's call noise suppression** uses the phone's own voice-call processing. It can help in
  noise but sounds less natural.
- **Reset** returns to your test result (or to the default if you haven't taken the test).

## Captions

*For profound hearing loss: speech around you as large text.*

1. Open **Captions** and choose the **speech language**, the language people around you speak.
   It can differ from the app's language.
2. Tap **Start captions**. Text appears as people speak; the word being heard now is shown in colour.
3. Use the **A–A** slider to make the text bigger or smaller. **Clear** removes the text.

Captions use your phone's speech-recognition service (usually Google). It may need an internet
connection, or an offline speech pack for your language from the phone's settings. The hearing
aid pauses while captions are on, because most phones give the microphone to one feature at a time.

## Working in the background

The hearing aid keeps running with the screen off or while you use other apps. More ›
**Background & shortcuts** has these options:

- **Notification buttons**: while it is on, the notification shows the situation and volume, with
  **Quieter**, **Louder** and **Turn off** buttons, also on the lock screen.
- **Quick Settings tile**: swipe down from the top of the screen twice, tap the pencil and drag the
  **Hearing aid** tile in. Tap it to switch on or off from anywhere; while on, it shows the volume.
- **Start when the app opens**: turns the hearing aid on as soon as you open the app, if earphones
  are connected.
- **Keep it running**: some phones (Xiaomi, Huawei, Samsung and others) close background apps to
  save battery. Tap **Open battery settings** and set Hearing Aid to *Unrestricted* or *Not optimized*.

If earphones are unplugged while it is on, sound pauses at once so the loudspeaker never howls, and
continues when they are back.

## Appearance

More › **Appearance**:

- **Light or dark**: same as the phone, always light, or always dark.
- **Colour skin**: Teal, Ocean, Sunset, Forest, Berry, **High contrast** (black and yellow in dark
  mode) and, on Android 12 and newer, **Wallpaper colours**.
- **Text size**: Normal, Large or Extra large, on top of the phone's own font size.

## Language

The app follows the phone's language. To choose another, use **More › Language** (or the picker in
the first safety notice). On Android 13 and newer you can also choose it in the phone's
Settings › Apps › Hearing Aid › Language. Persian, Arabic, Sorani Kurdish and Urdu use a
right-to-left layout.

Available languages: English, فارسی, العربية, کوردی (سۆرانی), Kurdî (Kurmancî), Türkçe,
中文 (简体), Deutsch, Русский, Italiano, Français, Español, Português, हिन्दी, اردو, 日本語, 한국어,
Bahasa Indonesia, Українська.

## About, updates and license

More › **About & updates** shows the version, author, e-mail, source code, website and license.
**Copy details** copies the version, Android version and phone model for bug reports.

The GitHub version checks GitHub for a newer release shortly after it opens (at most every 12
hours) and shows a banner at the top of Listen. **Later** hides the banner until the next version;
*Check for updates automatically* turns the check off. **Install** downloads the new version,
checks it against the release's `SHA256SUMS.txt`, and hands it to Android's installer. The first
time, Android asks you to allow installs from this app. Your settings and hearing test are kept.
The Google Play version gets its updates from Play instead.

The license is [PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0 with an additional permission for education](../LICENSE.md):
free for personal, non-commercial and educational use; commercial use needs a license from the
author.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| START is greyed out | Connect earphones. The card at the top says what is missing. |
| “Microphone permission is needed” | Allow the microphone in Settings › Apps › Hearing Aid › Permissions. |
| Loud whistle | See [Noise and whistling](#noise-and-whistling). |
| Voices don't match lips | You are on Bluetooth. Use wired earphones for no delay. |
| It stops after a while in the background | See *Keep it running* in [Working in the background](#working-in-the-background). |
| Sound is quiet even at full loudness | Raise the phone's media volume, and check *Maximum loudness* in Adjust. |
| Captions say “No internet” | Connect to the internet or download the offline speech pack for that language. |
| Captions say the language isn't supported | Choose another speech language, or install it in the phone's speech settings. |
| Update “didn't pass its safety check” | The download was damaged or incomplete. Try again later. |
| Android won't install an update | The app you have was not installed from these GitHub releases (for example an older test build). Uninstall it, then install the latest release. |

## Safety

- This app is **not a medical device** and does not replace a hearing professional.
- Start with low volume and raise it slowly. Loud sound for long periods damages hearing.
- See a doctor about sudden hearing loss, ear pain, dizziness or ringing in the ears.
- Use the app only to listen where you are allowed to. Secretly listening to or recording other
  people is illegal in many places and is not permitted by the [license](../LICENSE.md).

## Privacy

- Sound is processed on the phone in real time. The app never records, stores or sends it.
- Settings and the hearing test result stay on the phone.
- Captions use the phone's speech-recognition service, which may process audio under its own
  privacy policy. The app keeps the text only while the Captions screen is open.
- The GitHub version contacts GitHub only to check for and download updates. The app has no ads,
  no analytics and no accounts.
